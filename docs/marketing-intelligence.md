## 2. Socioeconomic Certifications

### Why These Certifications Matter to Federal Buyers

Federal agencies are required by law to meet annual small business contracting goals. These goals are broken down by socioeconomic category. Contracting officers who fail to meet these targets face program-level scrutiny. For a federal buyer choosing between two vendors of equal technical quality, the one that helps them meet a mandatory goal has a decisive advantage.

ISS holds **both** WOSB and SDVOSB certifications simultaneously — a rare combination that gives buyers access to two separate set-aside pools from a single vendor.

### WOSB — Women-Owned Small Business

**Certification:** WOSB (Women-Owned Small Business) under the SBA's WOSB Federal Contract Program

**What it enables:**
- Contracting officers may award WOSB set-aside contracts without full and open competition
- Available in NAICS codes where women-owned businesses are "underrepresented" or "substantially underrepresented"
- Allows sole-source awards up to $4.5M (most services) / $7M (manufacturing)
- Helps agencies meet their **5% WOSB annual goal** (mandated by the Small Business Act)

**Who benefits:**
- Any federal agency CO trying to meet the SBA's 5% WOSB goal
- Primes with SB subcontracting plans that include WOSB targets
- Agencies under SBA scorecard pressure (GSA, DoD, DHS, VA, DOI all carry public SB scorecards)

**Procurement language:** "WOSB set-aside," "women-owned small business preference," "SB subcontracting plan — WOSB credit"

### SDVOSB — Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business

**Certification:** SDVOSB under the SBA's SDVOSB Program (verified through the SBA's Veteran Small Business Certification — VetCert)

**What it enables:**
- **VA's Vets First program**: The Department of Veterans Affairs is legally required to consider SDVOSB and VOSB vendors *first* before any other procurement vehicle — before full and open competition, before existing contracts
- Non-VA agencies may use SDVOSB set-asides to meet their **3% SDVOSB goal**
- Allows sole-source awards up to $4.5M (most services) / $7M (manufacturing) — same as WOSB
- Significant procurement advantage in DoD contracting, where veteran-owned preferences apply

**Who benefits:**
- **VA is the anchor opportunity**: The VA's Vets First mandate effectively requires SDVOSB-first consideration. ISS's veteran-led background and VA-aligned mission make this a natural fit.
- Defense primes with veteran subcontracting targets
- Any agency CO meeting the 3% SDVOSB mandate

**Procurement language:** "SDVOSB set-aside," "Vets First," "VetCert-verified," "SDVOSB sole source," "service-disabled veteran preference"

### Dual Certification — The Combined Advantage

ISS is one of a limited pool of firms that simultaneously satisfies both the WOSB and SDVOSB procurement categories. A prime contractor building a subcontracting plan can count ISS dollars toward *two separate socioeconomic credit lines* — WOSB and SDVOSB — which is a significant administrative and compliance advantage when building teaming relationships.

**Key talking point for BD conversations:**
> "Working with ISS lets your program meet Women-Owned and Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned targets simultaneously. That's two separate SBA scorecard credits from a single vendor relationship."

## 9. Market Intelligence and Opportunity Landscape

### 9.1 Federal Small Business Market Context

The federal government is the world's largest buyer of goods and services — over $700B in annual contract spending. Roughly 23% of that total (the SBA's statutory goal) is designated for small businesses, which equates to approximately $161B annually. Within that pool:

- **5% WOSB goal**: ~$35B annually directed toward Women-Owned Small Businesses
- **3% SDVOSB goal**: ~$21B annually directed toward Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Businesses
- **Overlap opportunity**: Firms holding both WOSB and SDVOSB certifications can compete in both pools

Many agencies run behind on their WOSB goals — GSA, DoD, HHS, and Treasury have historically published scorecards showing shortfalls. This creates urgency for contracting officers to find qualified WOSB vendors in their NAICS codes.

### 9.2 VA as the Anchor Opportunity

**Why the VA matters disproportionately for ISS:**

The Veterans Benefits, Health Care, and Information Technology Act of 2006 — implemented through the "Vets First" rule — requires the Department of Veterans Affairs to consider SDVOSBs and VOSBs ahead of all other procurement methods. This is not a preference. The VA's rule of two requires contracting officers to conduct a SDVOSB/VOSB set-aside when there is a reasonable expectation of two or more qualified bids at fair market price.

The VA is one of the largest IT buyers in the federal government, with a multi-year Electronic Health Record Modernization (EHRM) program, dozens of IT service task orders, and active modernization work across the VBA (Veterans Benefits Administration) and VHA (Veterans Health Administration).

**ISS's positioning at the VA:**
- SDVOSB status: legally mandated first consideration
- Mission alignment: ISS leadership background in veteran-serving programs aligns with VA culture
- Enterprise IT modernization: directly relevant to VA's EHRM and platform modernization programs
- Program management credentials: VA programs are heavily PM-intensive

**Target vehicles:**
- VA T4NG (T4 Next Generation) — the primary VA IT IDIQ, multiple task order pools
- VA BPA instruments under T4NG
- VA VOSB sole-source awards under $4.5M

## 11. Website and Content Marketing Recommendations

### 11.1 What Works on High-Performing Govcon Websites

Research across leading small business govcon sites (and competitors) shows consistent patterns in what earns credibility with federal buyers:

**Evidence over claims:**
- Named agencies, not "a federal agency" — govcon buyers are suspicious of vague references
- Specific outcomes: "20,000+ targets exploited," "retrieval times reduced from weeks to hours," "resolved legacy backlogs within 60 days"
- Named certifications: "PMP-certified" not just "experienced project managers"

**Procurement readiness signals:**
- UEI, CAGE code, SAM profile link — visible on the capability statement and credentials pages
- NAICS codes listed in full — contracting officers search by NAICS before reading anything else
- Downloadable capability statement — accessible from every page

**Trust through specificity:**
- Named past performance on the "Who We Serve" page — not just logos of past clients
- Certifications page that explains *what WOSB and SDVOSB mean for the buyer*, not just listing the certifications

**Honest qualification:**
- Pages that tell buyers when ISS is NOT the right fit build more trust than pages that claim universal capability
- The fit qualifier section on "Who We Serve" is a high-trust pattern used by premium govcon firms

### 11.2 Page Flow Recommendations

**Homepage flow:**
1. Hero — credential statement + primary CTA
2. Trust badges — socioeconomic certs + named credentials
3. Capability overview — 6 areas, icons, brief summaries
4. Past performance highlights — 3 named entries
5. Final CTA — with secondary download action

**Who We Serve flow:**
1. Hero — audience-specific value proposition
2. Certification strip — WOSB + SDVOSB procurement explanation (dark background, high visual weight)
3. Buyer segments — 5-tab group with agency tags and buyer concerns
4. Fit qualifier — explicit "right fit / not right fit" two-column panel
5. Named past performance — all 5 entries as cards
6. CTA — procurement-specific copy

**Capabilities flow (per capability page):**
1. Hero — buyer problem statement, not capability description
2. What's included — specific deliverables list
3. Buyer outcomes — what the buyer achieves
4. Ideal buyers — who this is for
5. Past performance tie-ins — which named programs demonstrate this
6. Related capabilities — cross-link to adjacent areas
7. CTA — "capability discussion" rather than generic "contact us"

### 11.3 Tone and Messaging Guidance

**Use this language:**
- "Past performance" not "experience" — govcon buyers think in past performance terms
- "Set-aside eligible" not "preferred" — buyers respond to procurement mechanics
- "Named delivery history" not "proven track record" — specificity over cliché
- "Mission tempo" and "accountability" in defense/intel context — resonates with operators
- "Chain of custody" in forensics context — legal precision signals competence

**Avoid this language:**
- "Innovative solutions" — table stakes, not differentiating
- "Client-focused delivery" — everyone says this
- "Trusted partner" — meaningless without specifics
- "A leading provider of..." — signals a staffing body shop, not a specialized firm

### 11.4 Capability Statement as a Marketing Asset

The downloadable capability statement at `/capability-statement/print` is underutilized. For govcon buyers, the capability statement is often the first artifact they look at before a vendor meeting. It should:

1. Be printable and formatted for the standard 1-2 page govcon format
2. Lead with UEI/CAGE/NAICS — COs need this to plug into procurement systems
3. Include past performance with named agencies and a one-sentence outcome for each
4. List all certifications: WOSB, SDVOSB, and professional credentials
5. Include contact information visible in the footer of both pages

The current ISS capability statement already does most of this. The primary enhancement opportunity is making the SAM profile link clickable in digital versions and ensuring the NAICS code list is current with the 18 codes registered.
