Not every teaming arrangement is strategic. Some are compliance exercises that create overhead without creating value. But when a prime contractor teams with a WOSB or SDVOSB IT firm at the right time, on the right vehicle, with the right structure — the arrangement unlocks contract opportunities that neither firm could access alone.

This guide identifies the specific conditions under which teaming with a WOSB or SDVOSB IT firm is a strategic advantage, how to structure the arrangement, and when it is better to compete full and open.

## The Strategic Triggers

### 1. The Agency Is Behind on Socioeconomic Goals

The federal government sets annual small business contracting goals under the Small Business Act (15 U.S.C. § 644):

| Category | Government-Wide Goal |
|---|---|
| **Total Small Business** | 23% of prime contract dollars |
| **WOSB** | 5% |
| **SDVOSB** | 3% |
| **Small Disadvantaged Business** | 5% |
| **HUBZone** | 3% |

The SBA publishes annual scorecards grading each agency's achievement. Agencies that are underperforming on WOSB or SDVOSB targets face scrutiny from Congress, SBA, and OMB — and they respond by increasing set-asides, adding small business evaluation factors, and scrutinizing subcontracting plans more rigorously.

**When to act**: Monitor the SBA scorecard (published at sba.gov). When an agency receives a poor grade on WOSB or SDVOSB achievement, expect more set-aside activity in that category — and position your team accordingly.

### 2. The Vehicle Has Small Business Pools

Many of the highest-value federal IT vehicles restrict competition on certain tracks or pools to small businesses. A large prime cannot compete on these pools alone, but can participate as a subcontractor to a small business prime or through a Mentor-Protégé joint venture.

| Vehicle | Agency | Small Business Pools | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8(a) STARS III** | GSA | 8(a) firms (many also WOSB/SDVOSB) | $50B |
| **OASIS+ SB** | GSA | WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone, 8(a) pools | Multiple domains |
| **CIO-SP4 SB** | NIH NITAAC | Small business GWAC with WOSB/SDVOSB holders | $20B+ |
| **Alliant 2 SB** | GSA | Small business GWAC | $15B |
| **Polaris** | GSA | WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone pools | Next-gen IT GWAC |
| **SEWP VI** | NASA | Small business group | IT products and product-based services |
| **VETS 2** | VA | SDVOSB-specific | $5B |

Even on unrestricted vehicles, individual task orders may be set aside for WOSB or SDVOSB firms under fair opportunity procedures. A large prime with no small business teaming partner is locked out of this work entirely.

### 3. The Subcontracting Plan Is at Risk

Under **FAR 19.702**, any contract over **$750,000** awarded to an other-than-small business must include a subcontracting plan with goals for small business categories — including WOSB and SDVOSB.

Failure to make a good-faith effort to meet these goals can result in:
- **Liquidated damages** under FAR 52.219-16 — assessed as the actual dollar shortfall from each goal
- **Negative past performance ratings** in CPARS
- **Reduced competitiveness** on future bids where small business participation is an evaluation factor

A prime that is consistently missing its WOSB or SDVOSB subcontracting targets has a concrete financial incentive to team with certified firms. A dual-certified partner — one holding both WOSB and SDVOSB — is especially efficient here, since subcontract dollars to that firm count toward both plan categories simultaneously.

### 4. Sole-Source Opportunities Under $4.5M

Both WOSB and SDVOSB programs provide sole-source authority to contracting officers:

| Program | Services Ceiling | Manufacturing Ceiling | FAR Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WOSB** | $4.5M | $7M | FAR 19.1506 |
| **SDVOSB** | $4.5M | $7M | FAR 19.1406 |

A large prime can identify requirements under the $4.5M threshold and support a WOSB/SDVOSB partner's sole-source proposal — with the large firm performing as a subcontractor. Unlike 8(a) sole-source awards, **SDVOSB sole-source does not require prior SBA approval** — the contracting officer has direct authority.

In IT, many task orders on existing vehicles fall under $4.5M, making this a high-volume opportunity channel.

## The Mentor-Protégé Advantage

The SBA's **Mentor-Protégé Program** (13 CFR 125.9) is the most powerful teaming structure available to large primes seeking set-aside access.

### How It Works

An approved Mentor-Protégé pair can form a **joint venture (JV)** that competes for set-aside contracts as a small business — even when one partner is large. This is possible because of an **affiliation waiver** under 13 CFR 121.103(h)(3)(ii): the JV's size is determined by the protégé alone, not the combined entities.

### Key Requirements

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Mentor limit** | No more than 3 protégés at a time |
| **Protégé limit** | Only one mentor at a time |
| **Agreement term** | 3 years, extendable to 6 |
| **JV managing venturer** | Must be the protégé |
| **Protégé work share** | At least 40% of the JV's work |
| **JV award limit** | 3 separate prime contract awards per 2-year period (unlimited orders under a single IDIQ) |
| **SBA approval** | Required before the relationship begins |

### The JV Structure

Most Mentor-Protégé JVs in federal IT use the **unpopulated model**: the JV is a pass-through LLC that subcontracts work to the mentor and protégé firms. The JV itself typically has no direct employees — the work is performed by personnel from both firms.

The protégé must be the **managing venturer**, meaning:
- Protégé personnel hold senior management positions (PM, contract manager)
- The protégé controls the JV's bank account and financial management
- The protégé manages the day-to-day government relationship

For IT contracts, this means the protégé firm's PM runs the program while the mentor provides technical depth, cleared personnel, infrastructure, and surge capacity.

## Limitations on Subcontracting: The Critical Constraint

Every WOSB/SDVOSB set-aside contract is subject to the **limitations on subcontracting** rule (13 CFR 125.6, FAR 52.219-14).

For **service contracts**: the small business prime must perform at least **50% of the cost of personnel** with its own employees or similarly situated subcontractors.

### The Similarly Situated Entity Exception

This exception is essential for structuring compliant teams:

- A **similarly situated entity** is a subcontractor that is small for the relevant NAICS code **and** holds the same socioeconomic status as the prime
- WOSB prime + WOSB sub = both firms' labor counts toward the 50% requirement
- WOSB prime + large business sub = only the WOSB prime's labor counts

**Practical implication**: A large prime subcontracting to a WOSB prime can perform no more than 49% of the personnel cost (unless the WOSB has similarly situated subs whose labor also counts toward the 50% threshold).

### Compliance Measurement

Compliance is measured at the **end of contract performance**, not at award time. But violations carry serious consequences:
- Termination for default
- Referral to SBA OIG
- Suspension or debarment
- Finding of non-responsibility for future awards

### The Ostensible Subcontractor Risk

If the large prime is performing the primary and vital requirements while the small business prime is essentially a pass-through, the SBA may find the two entities **affiliated** under the ostensible subcontractor rule (13 CFR 121.103(h)(4)). This would disqualify the team on the set-aside.

The test is substance, not form. COs and SBA evaluators look at who controls the work, who provides the key personnel, and who the government actually interacts with day to day.

## When NOT to Team

Teaming with a WOSB/SDVOSB firm is not always the right strategy. Consider competing full and open when:

1. **The solicitation has no small business evaluation factor** and the contract is under $750K (no subcontracting plan required) — there is no competitive advantage to teaming

2. **The prime already exceeds its subcontracting goals** through existing relationships — adding a new partner creates complexity without benefit

3. **The work requires large-business scale** — enterprise-wide network transformations, large weapons system integrations, or programs requiring hundreds of cleared personnel may not suit a small business prime structure

4. **The small business would be a pass-through** — if the WOSB/SDVOSB cannot genuinely perform 50% of the personnel cost, the arrangement creates ostensible subcontractor risk and potential fraud exposure

5. **Organizational conflict of interest** — if the prime is performing SETA/advisory work on the same program, teaming to compete for implementation work creates OCI issues regardless of the teaming structure

6. **No capable WOSB/SDVOSB partner exists** in the technical domain — teaming for compliance without genuine technical contribution creates performance risk. The best partners bring multi-domain capability and leadership with hands-on contract delivery experience, not just certification status

## How to Structure the Teaming Agreement

A well-structured teaming agreement protects both parties and creates the foundation for a productive working relationship.

### Essential Elements

| Element | What to Define |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Specific opportunity (solicitation number, agency, contract name) |
| **Roles** | Who is prime, who is sub; specific technical areas each party performs |
| **Workshare** | Percentage of total value allocated to each party; minimum guaranteed workshare for the small business |
| **Key personnel** | Named individuals from each firm; commitment terms |
| **IP and data rights** | Background IP ownership; foreground IP allocation; government data rights flow-down (DFARS 252.227-7013/7014 for DoD) |
| **Past performance credit** | How CPARS evaluations and contract references will be attributed |
| **Financial terms** | Payment flow-down timelines, fee/profit structure, B&P cost sharing |
| **Exclusivity** | Whether either party may team with competitors on this opportunity |
| **Duration** | Termination triggers; wind-down obligations |
| **Compliance** | What happens if the WOSB/SDVOSB loses certification or exceeds its size standard |

### Past Performance Credit

This is often the most contentious element. The prime contractor receives the CPARS evaluation. Subcontractors can request a separate assessment under **FAR 42.1503(h)**, but this is not guaranteed. Define in advance how both parties will build and cite past performance from the engagement.

## Real-World IT Teaming Patterns

### Cybersecurity

The most common teaming domain. Large primes hold unrestricted SOC operations contracts; WOSB/SDVOSB firms provide niche capabilities — vulnerability assessment, penetration testing, RMF compliance, or specialized tool administration. On set-aside task orders, the roles reverse: the WOSB/SDVOSB primes the work and the large firm provides cleared SOC analysts, 24/7 coverage, and enterprise security tooling.

### Cloud Migration

Under cloud-specific BPAs and task orders on OASIS SB or Alliant 2 SB, the WOSB/SDVOSB serves as prime and the large firm provides enterprise architecture, migration factory tooling, and program management. Many small WOSB/SDVOSB firms have developed strong AWS GovCloud and Azure Government expertise that complements a large prime's program management infrastructure.

### Application Development and Modernization

The Technology Modernization Fund, FITARA scorecard pressures, and agency CIO mandates drive significant application modernization spending through set-aside vehicles. WOSB/SDVOSB firms provide agile development teams and DevSecOps capability; large primes contribute systems engineering, testing, and ATO support.

### Data Analytics and Geospatial

Agencies with geospatial, intelligence analysis, or data visualization requirements increasingly route this work through set-aside vehicles. WOSB/SDVOSB firms with platform expertise in tools like Palantir, ArcGIS, or Tableau provide the analytical capability; large primes contribute data engineering infrastructure, enterprise integration, and security architecture. This domain rewards firms that combine analytics expertise with broader IT delivery capability — cloud, cybersecurity, and program support — under one team.

### Help Desk and End-User Services

Staff-intensive contracts that fit within WOSB/SDVOSB-eligible NAICS codes. The 50% personnel cost limitation is critical here — the small business must ensure its employees perform the majority of the labor. These contracts are often LPTA, which compresses margins and makes workshare allocation financially challenging.

### Common Structure Patterns

| Pattern | When to Use |
|---|---|
| **Large prime as sub to small prime** | Set-aside task orders; the WOSB/SDVOSB holds the vehicle position and manages the government relationship |
| **Mentor-Protégé JV as prime** | Larger set-aside contracts where the protégé needs the mentor's infrastructure and past performance |
| **Large prime as prime with small sub** | Unrestricted contracts where WOSB/SDVOSB participation earns evaluation credit and meets subcontracting plan goals |
| **Cascading teaming** | Large prime holds unrestricted IDIQ; when set-aside task orders emerge, the prime supports the WOSB/SDVOSB partner's bid on a different vehicle |

## Key Takeaways

- Team when the agency is behind on socioeconomic goals, when the vehicle has small business pools, or when sole-source authority under $4.5M creates an alternative acquisition path
- The Mentor-Protégé program's affiliation waiver lets a large prime and small protégé compete as small through a joint venture — the most powerful teaming structure available
- Limitations on subcontracting require the small business to perform at least 50% of personnel cost; the similarly situated entity exception allows WOSB-to-WOSB or SDVOSB-to-SDVOSB subcontracting to count
- Dual-certified partners (WOSB + SDVOSB) maximize subcontracting plan credit by satisfying two socioeconomic categories from a single relationship
- Do not team when the arrangement would make the WOSB/SDVOSB a pass-through — the ostensible subcontractor rule creates real disqualification risk
- Structure the teaming agreement to address workshare, IP, past performance credit, and compliance obligations before the proposal is due
- The strongest teaming arrangements pair the large prime's infrastructure and scale with a small firm that brings genuine multi-domain delivery capability, verifiable credentials, and set-aside eligibility — not certification alone
